Key decisions — why · proves · does not prove · production implication

1 · Code-based golden path, not a low-code platform

Why: control, testability, repeatability for engineering-owned tools. Proves: a 4th module can look like the 3rd — marginal cost falls with reuse. Doesn't prove: any replacement for connectors, Dataverse, DLP, or citizen development. Implication: custom fits engineering-owned apps; forms-over-data stays on Power Apps.

2 · One deep KYC slice + two thin modules

Why: depth shows regulated-workflow fit; thinness isolates the reusable spine. Proves: shared components and service patterns carry across modules. Doesn't prove: per-app production cost — the pilot must measure it. Implication: the reuse claim is structural, not yet economic.

3 · Central server-side permissions, transitions, validation

Why: one reviewable place for "who can do what" and "what may change into what." Proves: denials are testable first-class cases — a reviewer's flag toggle is refused by the server. Doesn't prove: SSO/MFA, record-level authz, segregation of duties. Implication: identity and fine-grained authz are pilot-blocking work.

4 · Audit event in the same transaction as every change

Why: "no change without a trail" as a structural guarantee. Proves: fault-injection test — if the audit write fails, the business write rolls back. Doesn't prove: tamper-evident retention; append-only holds at the app layer only. Implication: regulated flows need immutable storage + approved retention first.

5 · Repository-as-contract for Devin (AGENTS.md · module Skill · PR template) — human review as the merge gate

Why: encode the non-negotiable rules so agent output conforms by construction. Proves: with explicit conventions and tests, an agent can extend the path consistently. Doesn't prove: autonomy without oversight, or any build-time figure — the repository does not independently substantiate an end-to-end build-duration claim. Implication: budget for permanent human review and framework ownership (est. 0.5–1 FTE — assumption, to be measured in the pilot).

Bottom line: build-vs-buy is per-app, not tenant-wide. Custom wins only where engineering owns the tool in production, needs code-level control or deep internal integration, and the workflow is testable; Power Apps wins everywhere else — identity, governed data, connectors, DLP, and ALM come with the license. Largest unresolved risk: un-built production controls (SSO, record-level authz, source-of-truth integration, tamper-evident audit, monitoring) plus standing platform ownership can consume the ~$250K/yr ceiling. Next step: confirm the real seat mix behind the spend; take one pilot — feature-flag administration — to a true production bar and through security review; decide app #2 from measured hours, support burden, and lead time.
